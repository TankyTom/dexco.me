Privacy Policy

Last updated 6 November 2026

Tribar doesn’t collect, store or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it makes no network requests of its own. Everything it shows is read on your Mac and never leaves it.

What Tribar reads on your Mac

  • Battery, power and system statistics such as charge, battery health, CPU, memory, disk and network activity, to display them.
  • Which apps are using energy and memory, so it can list them and, if you turn it on, warn you about one that stays busy.
  • Wi-Fi status and nearby networks, so you can see and switch networks.
  • Paired Bluetooth devices and their battery levels, so it can show them and connect or disconnect them when you ask.

None of this is stored beyond what the app needs to draw its graphs while it’s running, and none of it is sent anywhere.

Location

macOS treats Wi-Fi network names as location information, so it only shows them to apps you’ve given Location access. Tribar asks for it solely to display network names. It never reads, stores or sends your location. You can decline, and everything else keeps working.

Wi-Fi passwords

If you type a Wi-Fi password in Tribar, it’s saved in your Mac’s own keychain so you aren’t asked again. It never leaves your Mac. You can remove every saved password in Tribar’s Settings → General → Saved Wi-Fi passwords.

Notifications

If you turn on energy warnings, Tribar posts a local notification on your Mac. Nothing is sent to a server.

Purchases

Tribar Pro is bought through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles the payment; Tribar only learns whether Pro is unlocked. Apple’s privacy policy covers your App Store account.

Children

Tribar collects no data from anyone, including children.

This website

This site uses no cookies or analytics. The hosting provider may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses, for security.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
